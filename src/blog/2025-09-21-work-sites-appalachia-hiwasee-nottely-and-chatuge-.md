<p class="MsoNormal">A quick(ish) 2 day loop starting at my favorite motorcycle
hangout Smitty’s lodge in Tellico Plains and riding to Appalachia, Hiwasee,
Nottely and Chatuge dams. But first...

<p class="MsoNormal">Bald River Falls!

<a href="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2451.JPEG" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><img border="0" data-original-height="2048" data-original-width="1536" height="640" src="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2451.JPEG" width="480" /></a>
<a href="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2447.JPEG" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><img border="0" data-original-height="1536" data-original-width="2048" height="480" src="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2447.JPEG" width="640" /></a>
  Now let us start with the biggest disappointment of the
trip. Appalachia isn’t a power generating dam, and it’s not even accessible. So
very much like Watauga and the fire training center, you get the gate view.<a href="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2431.JPEG" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><img border="0" data-original-height="1536" data-original-width="2048" height="240" src="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2431.JPEG" width="320" /></a>

<p class="MsoNormal">

A little bummed, but it was time to head to Nottely. Or so I thought. Not more than
three miles down the road I see a sign that says Dam that way. What Dam? I didn’t
see a dam on my plans in this area. But I am here and I know a sign when I see
one so turn I did! And rode. And rode. And rode and rode for almost 8 miles
before I came to what turned out to be Hiwasee dam. 

<a href="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2456.JPEG" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><img border="0" data-original-height="1536" data-original-width="2048" height="480" src="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2456.JPEG" width="640" /></a>

<p class="MsoNormal">Somehow this was left off
the list of every plan I had made but here I am! It’s a regal dam, beautiful in
it’s own right and with the first leaves of autumn starting to fall, turns out
to be quite picturesque.

Hiwassee has what was the only reversible pump turbine setup when it was
installed in 1956. Avid fishers love Hiwasee for small mouth bass and walleye. <o:p></o:p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Nottely

<a href="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2477.JPEG" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><img border="0" data-original-height="1536" data-original-width="2048" height="480" src="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2477.JPEG" width="640" /></a>
<o:p></o:p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Nottely dam is what is called a seasonal dam. We raise and
lower the pool depending on the season. Visiting at the beginning of fall you
can already see the lowering that has occurred in preparation for the heavy
rain expected in the fall/early winter season.

<a href="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2485.JPEG" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><img border="0" data-original-height="1536" data-original-width="2048" height="480" src="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2485.JPEG" width="640" /></a>
<a href="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2479.JPEG" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><img border="0" data-original-height="1536" data-original-width="2048" height="480" src="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2479.JPEG" width="640" /></a>

<p class="MsoNormal"> It is one of the smaller dams in
works repertoire pumping out about 15 megawatts. The dam was built with
specific reasons in mind, flood control being the biggest. 

<a href="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2492.JPEG" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><img border="0" data-original-height="1536" data-original-width="2048" height="480" src="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2492.JPEG" width="640" /></a>

<p class="MsoNormal">Because of that it
might look haphazardly designed with the bridge sort of over it and the
spillway location but it works perfect every time. <o:p></o:p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Chatuge

<a href="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2499.JPEG" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><img border="0" data-original-height="1536" data-original-width="2048" height="480" src="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2499.JPEG" width="640" /></a>

<p class="MsoNormal">The last site visited this summer. On the border of North Carolina
and Georgia. This 3 turbine dam pumps out 24 megawatts of power. 

<a href="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2506.JPEG" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><img border="0" data-original-height="1536" data-original-width="2048" height="480" src="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2506.JPEG" width="640" /></a>
Riding in I
was struck with the winding lazy road that took you up to the dam and the small
side road off to the pump house. You felt like you were entering a park not a
dam. I’m not complaining. <a href="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2501.JPEG" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><img border="0" data-original-height="2048" data-original-width="1536" height="640" src="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2501.JPEG" width="480" /></a>
Chatuge is also a seasonal dam. As you
can see the bowl level is also low at the time of visit.<a href="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2510.JPEG" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><img border="0" data-original-height="1536" data-original-width="2048" height="480" src="/assets/blog-images/IMG_2510.JPEG" width="640" /></a>
