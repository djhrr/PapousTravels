  Guntersville Dam is a classic TVA hydroelectric powerhouse. Construction began December 4, 1935 and the dam was completed in January 1939. It stands 94 feet high and stretches 3,979 feet across the Tennessee River. Four generating units give it a summer net dependable capacity of 124 megawatts. The reservoir it creates — Lake Guntersville — is Alabama’s largest with 67,900 acres of water and almost 890 miles of shoreline.<a href="/assets/blog-images/IMG_3315.JPG" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><img border="0" data-original-height="3024" data-original-width="4032" height="300" src="/assets/blog-images/IMG_3315.JPG" width="400" /></a>

<p class="MsoNormal">Riding across the top of the dam and down to the visitor area gives you that classic TVA feel — big concrete walls, spillway gates, and the massive reservoir behind it. Crappie is king here, with bass and sauger fishing also legendary. The bigger lock (added in 1965) keeps the commercial barges moving smoothly. It’s a working dam that still looks regal after all these decades.

Now for the biggest contrast of the day. Browns Ferry is TVA’s first and largest nuclear plant — three boiling water reactors on 840 acres beside Wheeler Reservoir near Athens, Alabama. When the units came online in the mid-1970s they were the largest in the world at the time, each capable of over 1,000 megawatts. Today, after uprates, the plant can produce up to 3,954 megawatts — enough electricity for more than two million homes and about 20% of TVA’s total generation.
